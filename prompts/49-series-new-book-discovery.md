# 49 · Find new books in followed series

## Goal

Help the reader discover newly cataloged or upcoming books in series they care about, distinguish them from older missing volumes, and choose which to add. Nothing enters the library or changes queue order without a preview and confirmation.

## Establish the baseline

Read `AGENTS.md`, `README.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, and `SECURITY.md`; search relevant team memory in `personal/next-chapter` and verify useful records against current code. Inspect prompts 36 and 44, `series.go`, `hardcover_catalog.go`, `handlers_hardcover_catalog.go`, their tests/templates, linking, aliases, migrations, restore, and plan/Undo handling.

The series dashboard already groups local books, shows reading progress and next known unread volumes, and displays cached Hardcover members outside the library. Catalog refresh is reader-initiated; adding selected cached members already has a preview and creates ordinary Needs placement books at the queue's end. Extend those paths. The current catalog query caps series membership at 100 and filters book status; inspect those limits before claiming complete discovery or support for upcoming releases. Current cache selection also relies on series names, which is insufficient for ambiguous names and multiple series memberships.

## Research and scope

Use current official Hardcover API documentation and the [published schema](https://github.com/hardcoverapp/hardcover-docs/blob/main/schema.graphql) to verify stable series IDs, canonical/merged IDs, membership pagination, book statuses, release dates and their precision, ordering, and required scopes. Record dated evidence. Do not infer publication from an edition's date or a release year alone. Mark unsupported upcoming-release information as unknown; do not invent release dates or claim a catalog is exhaustive.

## Task

1. **Follow a series.** Let the reader explicitly follow/unfollow series represented in the library, including series whose local books are all read. Use confirmed Hardcover series IDs with display names, not names alone. Let a linked book with multiple series memberships expose the choices; resolve ambiguous names with a preview. Preserve local grouping and ranking rules, and keep local-only series usable when Hardcover is disconnected. Following does not change book metadata or add missing volumes.
2. **Check for new books.** Add a reader-initiated check for one followed series or a bounded selection. Show the estimated request cost, last successful check, source, and freshness. Fetch all pages within a documented budget; if a series is incomplete or a check stops early, label it partial and allow continuation. Reuse the existing authenticated client and rate limiter. No requests on dashboard load, no scheduled polling, notifications, email, or new mandatory service in this version.
3. **Remember what was seen.** Store enough series/member identity and discovery history to distinguish “first catalog check,” “new since your previous complete check,” and “not in your library.” A first check establishes a baseline and shows existing missing volumes without labelling every old book a new release. Preserve unresolved discoveries until acknowledged, added, or dismissed; checking again must not make them vanish. Partial/failed checks must not advance a complete-check baseline or imply a missing remote member was deleted. Explain a clear first-check/acknowledgement policy.
4. **Useful discovery results.** Show newly discovered members, already known missing volumes, in-library books, and upcoming releases where supported. “Newly discovered” describes catalog discovery, not proof of recent publication. Display title/author, series position, source link, release information with its actual precision, and why the book appears. Keep fractional/null positions, duplicate numbers, prequels, novellas, omnibuses, and alternative reading orders visible without treating them all as mandatory prerequisites. Sort deterministically and do not infer an unwritten book from a numeric gap.
5. **Deduplicate safely.** Match known Hardcover IDs first, then existing validated identifiers/aliases and conservative title/author rules. Distinguish another edition from a new work. Account for the same book in multiple followed series, catalog merges/corrections, locally merged books, and remembered deletions. A deleted/dismissed book must not be recreated or repeatedly offered as new; provide an explicit way to revisit dismissed results. Ambiguous matches require reader review rather than automatic links or merges.
6. **Add selected books.** Reuse and extend the existing cached-member preview instead of adding a parallel import path. Explain title, author, series, position, release status, and resulting local status. Confirmation adds eligible selections once as normal Needs placement books at the queue's end, with confirmed Hardcover links and no silent reordering. Recheck local revision, follow/series identity, cache version, aliases, and existing books before applying; changed selections require a fresh preview. Do not push a shelf/status to Hardcover merely because a discovery was added locally. Unfollow/dismiss/check never changes the library or queue.
7. **Persistence and offline behavior.** Persist follows, dismissals/acknowledgements, and discovery state deliberately; distinguish durable reader choices from disposable catalog cache. Export/restore durable choices with validation and older-export compatibility. Define merge/delete, disconnect, Forget Hardcover ids, account changes, and Undo behavior; do not leak discoveries across the wrong series identity or erase later acknowledgements via an unrelated queue Undo. Render cached results offline with an honest timestamp and retain good cache data after failed refreshes.
8. **Accessible interface.** Integrate with the existing Series page, search, pagination, and notices. Show empty states for no follows, no new books, unlinked/ambiguous series, missing scopes, stale/partial results, and disconnected Hardcover. Bound large follow lists and avoid per-book queries. Preserve server rendering, no-JavaScript actions, CSP, CSRF, authentication, and plain CSS. Do not send private notes, quotes, reviews, or the full reading history to catalog endpoints.

## Acceptance criteria

- Synthetic fixtures cover first versus later checks, a newly added catalog member, an older missing volume, an announced future release, unknown/partial dates, repeated checks, dismiss/revisit, and follow/unfollow.
- Test more than 100 members, pagination and continuation, budget exhaustion, 429/timeout/schema errors, and partial results without false completeness or lost discoveries. Checking/page rendering never creates books or changes queue order.
- Test same-name series with different IDs, multiple series per book, canonical-ID changes, editions, duplicate IDs across series, fractional/null/duplicate positions, gaps, deleted aliases, existing unlinked matches, and ambiguous identity.
- Preview/apply tests cover duplicate submissions, new local matches, stale cache/revision, changed follow identity, and idempotent additions with Needs placement and stable existing queue order. Catalog checks must not alter ranking inputs, status, ratings, progress, or reading history.
- Cover existing caches, migrations, export/restore, disconnect/Forget ids, merge/delete, and unrelated Undo. Append the next available migration without editing `schema.sql` or shipped migrations; review `maxRestoreSchema` and document automatic backup and rollback. Do no network work inside `Store.Write`; respect single-connection SQLite query/transaction constraints.
- Check desktop/narrow mobile in light/dark and capture synthetic screenshots. Run required checks from `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Update README with follow/check/add steps, first-check semantics, dismissals, release-date limitations, catalog completeness, and request budgets.

Implement the feature using verified capabilities. Finish with the reader workflow, API sources and limitations, changed files, and validation results. Distinguish synthetic verification from any live API check; do not modify a live account during development without explicit authorization.
